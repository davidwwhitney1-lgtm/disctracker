DiscTracker Housing

The pod clicks into a dock glued under the flight plate. Three hooks on the dock snap over a ridge near the bottom of the pod. A tab on the pod and a notch in the dock make sure the USB-C side always faces the same way. Drag to turn, scroll to zoom.

How it works

Click in

Line up the tab with the dock's notch and push. The three hooks ride up the ridge's ramp and snap under it. The catch face is nearly flat, so the pod won't shake loose when the disc hits a tree or chains.

Take out

Hook a fingernail under each arm's small outer lip, ease it outward, and pull the pod down. The arms flex outward only, so they never press on the board.

Inside the pod

  • The board sits on three 2 mm pins through its alignment holes; melt or glue the pin tips.
  • The sensors face the lid, with 0.17 mm of clearance.
  • The battery and XIAO face the floor cover, with 0.3 mm of clearance. Add a foam pad on the battery.
  • The cover clicks in on three bumps. It has a sound hole for the buzzer, a window for the XIAO's LEDs, and a fingernail notch.

Gluing the dock

Use 3M VHB with 3M 94 primer. The printed jig sits on the upturned disc's rim and holds the dock dead center. Its front pointer lines up with the disc's stamp. Set DISC_OD and RIM_TO_PLATE in housing.py for each disc mould.

Files

SolidWorks

Open the .step files in hardware/cad/out/: pod, cover, dock, jig, and housing_assembly.step with the board in place.

Printing

.stl files in the same folder. Order the pod, cover and dock in nylon PA12 (MJF or SLS). Print the jig in PETG or PLA at home; it's 216 mm across.

Changing it

Every size is a parameter at the top of hardware/cad/housing.py (CadQuery). Re-run it to rebuild all files.
