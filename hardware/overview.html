Does the XIAO's position affect the measurements?

No. In the new design the XIAO is only the computer and radio. It doesn't do any of the measuring.

U1, the center sensor, stays at the exact center of the board, and all five sensors sit on the same face in the same orientation. The spin-pair math only needs the sensors' positions relative to each other, and those are exact (20.00 mm).

The XIAO's own motion chip ends up about 15 mm off center. It is only used to wake the disc up, so that doesn't matter.

The mounted face does flip the sensor axes (up becomes down). Firmware fixes that with a sign change.

Calculated The thing that does matter is how well the pod is centered on the disc's spin axis. A disc spins around its own balance point, so this includes any imbalance in the disc or the pod. The spin pair cancels this offset completely. The center sensor doesn't: each 0.1 mm off center reads as about 0.7 g of fake sideways pull at 2,500 rpm. That offset stays fixed relative to the board, while real drag sweeps around once per spin, so the firmware can tell them apart. Plan: a centering jig when gluing the dock, plus a quick one-time spin calibration per disc.

1 · How everything connects

Pin numbers on the motion chips now come from ST's datasheet (DS14623 Rev 1). The XIAO talks to the five motion chips over a shared SPI bus (copper), with one chip-select wire each (blue). The magnetometer is on I²C (purple). The buzzer shares D6 with U5 through JP1.

ConfirmedLSM6DSV320X pins: 1 SDO → MISO · 2 SDx and 3 SCx → GND · 4 INT1 → D7 (U1 only) · 5 VDDIO and 8 VDD → 3V3 with 100 nF each · 6, 7 GND · 9 INT2, 10 OCS_aux, 11 SDO_aux → solder pad only, no connection · 12 CS · 13 SCL/SPC → SCK · 14 SDA/SDI → MOSI.
ConfirmedSupply 1.71–3.6 V (3.3 V is fine) · SPI up to 10 MHz · high-g range ±32/64/128/256/320 g at 480–7,680 Hz · gyro up to ±4,000 dps · package 2.5 × 3.0 × 0.83 mm · about 0.9 mA per chip with everything on.
ConfirmedEach chip-select pin has a built-in pull-up at power-on. But the datasheet's I²C-disable bit, which firmware must set so the idle chips ignore SPI traffic, also switches that pull-up off. So I added R4–R8, 100 kΩ external pull-ups.
ConfirmedBuzzer CUI CPT-9019S-SMT: 9 × 9 × 1.9 mm piezo, 3 V / 5 mA, 65 dB at 10 cm, 4 kHz, driven straight from D6 (JLCPCB C95163). Q1 is an optional footprint in case it's too quiet.
ConfirmedXIAO charges at 50 mA by default (100 mA optional). So the battery should be ≥ 50 mAh to keep charging at or below 1C.
OpenFootprint: KiCad's built-in LGA-14 2.5 × 3 mm pattern (the one used for the LSM6DS3) should fit. I'll check it against ST technical note TN0018 when I build the library.

2 · Both faces of the board

To scale in millimetres, both seen from the same side so the coordinates line up. The other face is shown faded. The board grows to 56 mm so the battery can sit beside the XIAO instead of on top of it. That is where most of the height saving comes from.

DecidedSensors unchanged: U1 at the center, pairs at ±20.00 mm, same orientation, U4/U5 not fitted. The magnetometer moves to (0, −24), about 16 mm from the XIAO and 20 mm from the battery. It is clear of both, as its datasheet asks.
RecommendedXIAO shifted to about +15 mm so its USB-C port reaches the pod's edge. Then you charge and update firmware with a normal cable, even with the pod in the disc. The battery sits opposite to balance it.
CalculatedBalance: XIAO about 3 g × 15 mm vs battery about 2 g × 18 mm, with the trim pad and battery position closing the gap. Target: under 10 g·mm leftover imbalance. That shifts the disc's balance point by under 0.06 mm.
CalculatedAt 2,500 rpm the battery feels about 110 g outward (about 2 N for a 2 g cell) and the buzzer about 150 g. Both must be bonded down, not just taped.
EstimateBoard weight: a solid 56 mm, 0.8 mm board is about 3.6 g. Cutouts or 0.6 mm board material bring it to about 2.5 g.
OpenExact XIAO offset (set by the USB-C notch), battery part, and trim-pad size. These are final once the XIAO height and weight and the battery are measured.

3 · Dock and pod

Every disc gets a light dock: a thin ring glued permanently under the flight plate. The pod holds all the electronics and twists into any dock with a quarter turn, clicking into place. A wider key lug means it only fits one way round, so "front" is the same every time.

Estimate These need weighing. Under PDGA's ≈ 175 g limit, the disc itself should be ≈ 160 g or lighter.

CalculatedHeight ≈ 8 mm below the plate, against about 11 mm rim depth on the shallowest drivers (putters are 12–16 mm). That leaves about 3 mm of clearance, plus a little more from the plate's dome.
CalculatedTwist-lock load: spinning up to 2,500 rpm in about 0.1 s puts about 0.02 N·m of twist on the pod. A small snap detent easily holds that. Impacts are the real test: a 12 g pod at 1,000 g of shock pulls about 120 N. A 66 mm ring of tape holds several times that.
EstimateDisc plastics are slippery polyethylene/TPE blends that tape barely sticks to. Use 3M VHB with 3M 94 adhesion primer, or a plastic-bonding glue. Test peel strength on a spare disc first.
OpenTwist-lock vs three snap-in clips; print material (PETG, nylon or resin); USB-C notch size; LED window (translucent print); sound hole; splash sealing.

4 · Housing CAD brief

Dock (one per disc)

  • Flat ring, about 66 mm outside, about 50 mm opening, 0.8 mm thick, glued face flat (or curved to match the dome)
  • 3 bayonet lugs about 30–33 mm out, 2.6 mm tall, with an inward lip. One lug 14° wider as the key
  • Detent notch in each lug lip. Arrow marking "front"
  • Target ≤ 1.5 g. Print flat, lip printed as an overhang or bridged

Pod (one, moves between discs)

  • About 58 mm outside, about 7 mm tall, 0.6 mm lid and floor, 0.8 mm wall
  • 3 tabs on the top edge to match the lugs, each with a detent bump. Grip ribs on the side to twist
  • USB-C notch in the wall at "front". Open floor or translucent window under the XIAO LED. Sound hole under BZ1
  • Board held by 3 pins through the board's alignment holes, then potted or bonded. Battery bonded down

Centering jig

  • Printed ring that sits on the disc's outer edge (all discs ≈ 21.0–21.3 cm) with three flexible fingers
  • Center cup holds the dock while the glue sets. Aim for ±0.3 mm
  • Marks to line "front" up with the disc's stamp, so every disc is set up the same way

The pod is designed around the board's 3D model, which KiCad exports as a STEP file. So the order is: KiCad layout → STEP → pod CAD. The dock and the jig can start now.

5 · What's left before KiCad

  1. Measure the XIAO: total height including USB-C, and weight. Calipers and a gram scale. you
  2. Measure your discs: rim depth and inside rim diameter of your shallowest driver, plus weights. you
  3. Pick the battery: about 3 mm thick, ≥ 50 mAh, with protection circuit, footprint up to 15 × 20 mm. you choose · I'll shortlist
  4. OK the layout choices: 56 mm board, XIAO shifted to +15 mm with edge USB-C, dock + twist-lock pod. you
  5. Get the XIAO footprint from Seeed's KiCad library and find where its BAT pads land on our board. me
  6. Check the LGA-14 footprint against TN0018, and import the MMC5603NJ and CPT-9019S footprints from JLCPCB. me
  7. Check JLCPCB stock for the LSM6DSV320X, or plan to send the chips to them yourself. me

Items 5–7 are library work I can do as part of the KiCad step. Items 1–4 need you. Once they're in, the schematic is straightforward because every pin is now known.
