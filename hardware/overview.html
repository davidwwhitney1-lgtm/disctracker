1 · How everything connects

The XIAO talks to the five motion chips over one shared SPI bus (three wires, copper below). Each chip gets its own chip-select wire (blue) so the XIAO can talk to one at a time. The magnetometer uses a separate two-wire I²C bus (purple). The buzzer shares pin D6 with U5 through a solder jumper.

DecidedPin map: D8/D10/D9 = SPI, D0 D1 D2 D3 D6 = chip selects, D7 = data-ready, D4/D5 = magnetometer. Every XIAO pin D0–D10 is used.
DecidedBuzzer on D6. JP1 is a 3-pad solder jumper: bridge A to use U5, bridge B to use the buzzer. With pair B unfitted, it ships bridged to B.
ConfirmedMMC5603NJ: balls A1 = GND, A2 = SCL, B1 = VDD, B2 = SDA. Runs on 1.62–3.6 V, so 3.3 V is fine. Fixed I²C address 0x30, which doesn't clash with anything on that bus. 1,000 Hz only in its high-power mode.
ConfirmedMMC5603NJ wants at least 2.2 µF right at VDD. The old BOM said 1 µF, so C11 is now 2.2 µF. Datasheet pull-up guide: 2.7 kΩ for buses under 10 cm, 10 kΩ under 5 cm. Ours is about 3 cm, so 4.7 kΩ is fine.
ConfirmedLSM6DSV320X ranges: accelerometer up to 320 g with a 256 g option, high-g rate 480–7,680 Hz, gyro up to 4,000 dps (= 667 rpm). Source: Zephyr's driver bindings, because ST's site blocked the PDF from here. Re-check in the datasheet.
CalculatedSPI load: about 20 bytes per chip per reading. At 8 MHz that's about 25 µs each, or about 125 µs for all five, out of the 521 µs between readings at 1,920 Hz. That leaves plenty of room.
OpenLSM6DSV320X pin numbers and footprint, and how to tie off SDx, SCx and OCS_Aux in SPI mode. The sister chip LSM6DSV says: SDx/SCx to VDDIO or GND, OCS_Aux unconnected. Needs confirming for the 320X.
OpenWhether a bare piezo on a GPIO pin is loud enough, or Q1 (a small transistor) is needed. This depends on which piezo part you pick.

2 · Where the parts sit on the board

Drawn to scale in millimetres, looking at the sensor face. The XIAO is on the other face, shown dashed. That split matters (see the callout below).

New finding: sensors and XIAO go on opposite faces

The plan said "XIAO soldered flat, centered over U1". A XIAO soldered flat by its edge pads sits about 0 mm off the board, so the 0.86 mm-tall U1 can't fit under it. Put all the sensors on one face, which JLCPCB can machine-assemble, and hand-solder the XIAO on the other. All five chips stay on the same face in the same orientation, so the spin math is unchanged. It also means the true center can't have a drilled alignment hole, because U1 sits there. The drawing uses three holes on a 22 mm circle instead.

Decided48 mm round. U1 at center, U2/U3 at ±20.00 mm on X, U4/U5 at ±20.00 mm on Y. All the same rotation.
CalculatedAt 2,500 rpm each spin-pair chip feels 140 g (ω²r at 20 mm). Add roughly 20 g of pull and you get about 160 g, inside the 256 g range.
OpenMagnetometer spot. The datasheet says keep it away from current-carrying traces, including traces on the opposite face, and from the battery. At (−15, 15) it is only about 8 mm from the XIAO's corner. We need to find where the XIAO's charger chip and battery traces are, then move U6 (maybe to about r = 21 mm).
OpenXIAO underside battery-pad positions, which set where BAT+/BAT− land. Read them from Seeed's KiCad footprint.
OpenBalance. The XIAO is heavier at its USB-C end and the buzzer is off-center. Trim pads are drawn opposite each one, but their size comes from a mass estimate we haven't done yet.

3 · What the housing might look like

A side cut through the middle with the disc right-side up. The tracker hangs under the center of the flight plate. Heights are a first guess, roughly to scale.

EstimateStack height ≈ 15–16 mm below the plate, diameter ≈ 54 mm. Most of the height is the battery and the XIAO's USB-C connector.
EstimateHousing weight. A fully closed printed shell at 1 mm walls is about 6–9 g, heavier than the electronics. Ways to cut it: drop the lid and let the flight plate be the lid (glued ring), use 0.8 mm walls, or pot the board in a thin open cup. Target 3–5 g.
EstimateTotal add-on ≈ 10–14 g (board + XIAO + battery + housing). PDGA's limit for a 21.1 cm disc is about 175 g, so to stay legal a host disc would need to be around 160 g or lighter.
OpenDoes 15–16 mm clear the rim? Measure the inside depth of the discs you'll use. Drivers are shallow, and the housing must not hit the ground before the rim does.
OpenBattery size (mAh, dimensions) and runtime. The power draw of three IMUs at 1,920 Hz plus Bluetooth hasn't been worked out.
OpenHousing details still to design: material (PETG or nylon), USB-C opening, a window for the XIAO LED, a sound hole for the buzzer, how the lid holds at 2,500 rpm, and some splash resistance.

4 · What "16 MB of flash" means

Flash is the disc's own storage, the same kind of memory as a USB stick. The XIAO already has 2 MB, which your raw-data recorder uses. Throw results are tiny (20 bytes each), so you'll never fill flash with those. Flash only matters for recording every raw sensor sample so you can analyze throws later in disclab.py.

The new board makes far more raw data: three chips at 1,920 readings a second, plus the magnetometer, comes to about 113 KB every second. Today's board makes 5.4 KB/s, about 20 times less. Earlier notes estimated about 50 minutes for 16 MB, but that used today's rate.

5 · Where things stand

Known now

  • Chip positions and orientation
  • Full pin map, buzzer on D6
  • MMC5603NJ pinout, cap, address, layout rules
  • LSM6DSV320X ranges and rates (second source)
  • Spin forces: 140 g at 2,500 rpm
  • SPI bus has room for all five chips
