    Record raw data to this computer

    Streams every sensor reading (416 a second) over Bluetooth into a folder on this computer. Nothing is stored on the disc, so the length is limited only by this computer's disk – about 18 MB an hour. Every throw is also saved with its own readings and the disc's results. A new file starts every minute, so a crash loses at most a minute. Keep this page open (it can be in the background) and the computer awake.

    Not recording

    Tap a label before each test to mark it in the recording. To analyze: py disclab.py import "that folder".

    Calibrate the disc

    Step by step, with the disc on a table: which way is up on its board, the gyro's offset and the spin radius (how far the sensor sits from the disc's center). The calibration page connects to the disc itself, so this page lets go of it while you are there.

    DiscTracker Sim

    Every throw flies on this screen automatically.

    1. Wake the disc (pick it up or give it a shake). Close the phone app if it's connected.
    2. Click Connect and choose DiscTracker.
    3. Throw. Spin and wobble are measured while the disc flies freely, so give it a moment in the air: a toss caught from 1.5 m (5 ft) works, and 3 m (10 ft) or more from a net gives a longer free flight.

    Right now – straight from the chip

    The chip sends whole numbers ("counts"). Converting is a fixed scale: °/s = counts × 0.070, g = counts × 0.000488. Gyro tops out at ±2294 °/s (382 rpm), accelerometer at ±16 g.
